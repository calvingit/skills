# Teaching principles

Use these distinctions when choosing exercises, practice cadence or real-world
follow-up. Course paths and records are governed by [Teach](../SKILL.md).

## Knowledge, skills and wisdom

Knowledge comes from trusted sources and should include only what the target
skill requires. During acquisition, unnecessary difficulty consumes working
memory needed for understanding. A topic such as theoretical physics may need
more knowledge work; a topic such as yoga may need more skill practice.

Skills require durability and flexibility. Use interactive quizzes, browser tasks
or guided real-world steps with the tightest practical feedback loop, ideally
immediate and automatic. For quizzes, keep choices natural and do not reveal the
answer through conspicuously different formatting or detail.

Wisdom develops through applying skills with other learners and practitioners.
When a question needs practical judgement, first give the useful answer available,
then find a reputable community where the learner can test it. Respect a stated
preference against joining communities and retain that preference.

## Retention and challenge

Fluency is retrieval in the moment; storage strength is long-term retention.
Fluency can resemble mastery without establishing durable learning. Use retrieval
practice and spacing, and interleave different but related skills where useful.

Choose a challenge just beyond the learner's demonstrated understanding, grounded
in their mission and learning records. A short lesson should produce one tangible
win without overloading working memory.
